MTD quarterly updates are short summaries of your self-employment and property income and expenses that you send to HMRC from compatible software four times a year. Each one covers the tax year so far, and is due by 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May. For 2026/27, HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates, but you still have to send all four before you can file your tax return.

This guide walks through signing up, what goes into each update, the deadlines, how to submit, and what happens at the end of the year. It is general information, not tax advice.

What a quarterly update is

Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, sole traders and landlords keep digital records of their income and expenses and send HMRC a summary of those records every quarter. Making Tax Digital quarterly updates are not tax returns. You do not calculate profit, claim allowances or make accounting adjustments before you send one. You are simply telling HMRC the category totals your records show so far.

You send a separate update for each business. If you are self-employed and also let a UK property, that means one update for the self-employment and one for the property business each quarter.

An update is required even when nothing happened. If you had no income and no expenses in a period, you still send an update showing zero.

Before your first update: signing up

If you are asking how to sign up for Making Tax Digital, the short answer is that you use HMRC's online sign-up service, then connect your software. Since 6 April 2026, sign-up has been required for sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income from self-employment and property was over £50,000 on the tax return they filed the previous year. Qualifying income means turnover, measured before expenses. Anyone can also join voluntarily.

What you need to sign up

Before you start, check that you are registered for Self Assessment and have filed a tax return in the last two years. Then gather:

  • the Government Gateway user ID and password you use for Self Assessment and other HMRC online services
  • your National Insurance number
  • your Unique Taxpayer Reference; if you cannot find it, this guide on where to locate your UTR number explains where it appears on HMRC letters and in your online account
  • your business start date, or the date you started receiving property income, if this was in the last two tax years
  • for each self-employment: the business name you use on invoices, the business address and a description of your trade
  • the tax year you want to start using the service

You may also be asked to complete an identity check, for example using your passport or driving licence details. HMRC's sign-up guidance sets out the full list. If you use an accountant or tax agent, they can sign you up instead.

During sign-up, check every self-employment and property income source listed against your name and add any that are missing. Updates can only be sent for income sources HMRC knows about.

Authorising your software

Signing up tells HMRC you are in the scheme. Authorising your software lets it send data on your behalf. In your compatible software, choose the option to connect to HMRC, sign in with the same user ID and password you used to sign up, pass any identity check, and grant permission for the software to connect.

The connection lasts 18 months, after which you reconnect.

While setting up, check your update periods. The default follows the tax year. If you want calendar quarters, you must choose this before you send your first update.

What goes into a quarterly update

Your update contains totals for each income and expense category, taken from your digital records. You do not send receipts, invoices or bank statements.

Income

For a self-employment, income is mainly your turnover: sales, fees and other business takings. For a property business, it is rental income and any other property income.

Expense categories

Expenses are reported using the same categories as the Self Assessment return. For a self-employment these include cost of goods or stock, travel costs, office costs, staff costs and financial costs. Property businesses use categories such as rent, rates and insurance, and repairs and maintenance.

There is a simpler option for smaller businesses. If the turnover from a self-employment is under £90,000, you can choose simpler categorisation, which means you only need to record whether each transaction is income or an expense. Your update then shows one total for income and one total for expenses. The same applies to UK property businesses with total UK property turnover under £90,000, except that landlords of residential property must still identify residential finance costs separately. If you have several income sources, each one must be under the threshold to use this option for it.

If turnover for an income source reaches £90,000 during the year, you must go back and fully categorise its records from the start of the tax year before you can send your next update. If you are unsure whether you will cross the line, HMRC suggests categorising in full from the outset. The detail is in HMRC's guidance on creating digital records.

Cumulative figures

Updates are cumulative. Each one covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of the current quarter, not just the latest three months. Your second update for 2026/27, for example, covers 6 April to 5 October 2026. Because every update restates the year to date, any correction you make to earlier transactions simply flows into the next update's totals.

Standard and calendar quarters

Standard quarters match the tax year, ending on 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April. Calendar quarters end on the last day of June, September, December and March, which suits businesses whose accounting year runs from 1 April to 31 March. The deadlines are identical either way.

You elect for calendar quarters in your software before sending your first update of the tax year. Once you have sent an update, you cannot switch periods for the rest of that year.

How to submit an update step by step

  1. Bring your records up to date. Make sure every sale and expense up to the end of the period is recorded, including cash items and anything paid personally for the business.
  2. Categorise each transaction. Assign income and expenses to the right categories, or to income or expense if you use simpler categorisation.
  3. Check each business separately. Confirm the correct income source is selected for each update you are about to send.
  4. Review the totals. Compare the year to date figures with your bank statements and with your previous update. A sudden jump or drop is often a duplicate or a missed transaction.
  5. Submit through your software. Choose the option to send the quarterly update to HMRC and confirm.
  6. Keep the confirmation. Your software should show that HMRC received the update. You can also see submitted updates in your HMRC online account.

HMRC's page on sending quarterly updates confirms the periods and what each update must include.

Correcting mistakes

Because updates are cumulative, you usually do not need to resubmit an earlier one. Correct the transaction in your records and the next update will carry the right year to date totals.

Mistakes found after the fourth update are handled differently. You may need to resend the fourth update with corrected figures, and you should do this before making any end of year tax adjustments.

Late updates and penalty points

MTD for Income Tax uses a points-based system for late submissions. Each missed deadline earns one point. When you reach 4 points, you are charged a £200 penalty, and each further missed deadline while you are at the threshold brings another £200 penalty.

For 2026/27 there is a soft landing: HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates in this tax year. Points still apply if you file your tax return late. The relief does not remove the obligation either. You still need to keep digital records and send all four updates, because you cannot submit your tax return through software until they are in.

Points below the threshold expire after 24 months. Once you reach 4 points, you clear them by meeting every deadline for 12 months and making sure any outstanding submissions from the previous 24 months are sent. Late payment penalties are a separate system. HMRC's penalties guidance covers both.

After the fourth quarter

The fourth update closes the year's quarterly cycle, but it is not the end. You then finalise your figures for each business by making any accounting and tax adjustments, such as removing private use, adding capital allowances or adjusting for disallowable expenses. Next, you add any other income, such as employment, savings or dividends, and claim reliefs.

Finally, you submit your tax return through your software, sometimes still called the final declaration. For 2026/27 the deadline is 31 January 2028, which is also when any tax owed must be paid.

FAQ

Do I have to send a quarterly update if I had no income?

Yes. You must send an update for every quarter, even if there was no income or expenditure in the period. The figures will simply show zero.

Can I send my quarterly update early?

Yes. You can submit once the period has ended.

Will I be penalised for a late quarterly update in 2026/27?

No penalty points apply to late quarterly updates in the 2026/27 tax year. Late tax returns still earn points, and all four updates must be sent before you can file your return.

Do I still need to file a Self Assessment tax return?

Yes, but it is submitted through your MTD software instead of HMRC's online return. It brings together your adjusted business figures and any other income, and is due by 31 January after the end of the tax year.

Do I need a separate update for each business?

Yes. Each self-employment and each property business has its own quarterly update. A sole trader who also lets out a flat sends two updates every quarter.
