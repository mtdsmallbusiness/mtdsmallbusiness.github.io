Making Tax Digital for landlords means keeping digital records of your rental income and expenses and sending HMRC a summary every quarter, using compatible software. It has applied since 6 April 2026 to landlords whose gross property and self-employment income was over £50,000, and the threshold drops to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. What matters for landlords is how the threshold is measured: it is based on rent received before expenses, and joint owners count only their own share.

This is general information, not tax advice.

What MTD means for landlords

MTD for Income Tax does not change how much tax you pay on rental profits. It changes how you record and report them. Instead of pulling a year of bank statements and agent statements together once a year for a Self Assessment return, you keep a running digital record and send HMRC a cumulative update every three months. After the year ends, you finalise your figures and submit your tax return through the same software.

For most landlords the practical shift is timing. Records need to be up to date four times a year, not once.

Who has to join and when

HMRC looks at the Self Assessment return for an earlier tax year and checks your qualifying income against the threshold for that year:

  • From 6 April 2026: qualifying income over £50,000 on your 2024 to 2025 return.
  • From 6 April 2027: qualifying income over £30,000 on your 2025 to 2026 return.
  • From 6 April 2028: qualifying income over £20,000 on your 2026 to 2027 return.

So for making tax digital landlords 2026 is the first year for the largest portfolios, and the 2025 to 2026 return you file by 31 January 2027 decides whether you join in April 2027. HMRC writes to people it expects to be in scope, but you are expected to check your own figure whether or not a letter arrives. Some people can apply for an exemption, for example if they are digitally excluded, but exempt landlords still file a normal Self Assessment return.

How qualifying income is worked out for landlords

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. For a landlord that means gross rent, not profit. A landlord collecting £36,000 a year in rent with £20,000 of mortgage interest, repairs and agent fees has qualifying income of £36,000, not £16,000.

Several types of income are left out, including employment income under PAYE, pensions, dividends and your share of partnership profits. HMRC's page on working out your qualifying income lists the full set of inclusions and exclusions.

Jointly owned property

The rule for MTD landlords with jointly owned property is that each owner counts only their own share of the income. If two people own a let property equally and it brings in £50,000 a year, each has £25,000 of qualifying income from it. Whether each owner then joins MTD depends on that share plus any other property or self-employment income they have personally.

HMRC also says that if you only find out your share after expenses have been taken off, it will use that net figure.

Once you are in MTD, joint owners get some easing. You only need digital records of your own share of income and expenses, and for jointly let property you can choose to send quarterly updates showing income only, then add the expenses before you submit your tax return.

Landlords who are also self-employed

Rental income and self-employment turnover are added together for the threshold test. A landlord with £18,000 of rent and £15,000 from a sideline trade has £33,000 of qualifying income, even though neither source would be over £30,000 on its own.

Once in MTD, each business is reported separately. You send quarterly updates for each self-employment and for each property business, so someone with a trade and a UK let has two sets of updates to send each quarter.

Furnished holiday lets and overseas property

The furnished holiday lettings regime was abolished from 6 April 2025. Income from holiday lets and short-term lets is now taxed under the ordinary residential landlord rules, and it counts as UK or foreign property income for MTD.

Overseas lets matter in two ways. First, if you were UK tax resident, foreign property income counts toward qualifying income alongside UK rent. If you were not UK resident, only UK property income and income declared on your UK return count. Second, all your UK lets are treated as one UK property business, while all your overseas lets are treated as one separate foreign property business. A landlord with flats in Leeds and an apartment in Portugal therefore has two property businesses and two sets of quarterly updates.

What landlords have to do

Records for each property business

Each transaction needs a digital record showing the amount, the date, and a category that matches the property pages of the tax return. Because your UK lets form one business, you do not need separate records for each UK property.

Foreign property works differently. HMRC's guidance on keeping digital records says you need a separate record for each foreign property, and for your share of any jointly let foreign property, before your software combines them into one update.

Some simplifications help smaller landlords:

  • If total UK property turnover is under £90,000, you can use simpler expense categories. Residential landlords still need to separate out finance costs such as mortgage interest, because relief on those is restricted.
  • Spreadsheets are allowed, as long as they link digitally to software that sends the updates. Copying and pasting figures once they have been sent is not.
  • Records for a quarter need to exist before you send that quarter's update, but HMRC advises recording transactions as close to the date as you can.

Quarterly updates

A quarterly update is a summary of category totals, not a copy of every receipt. Updates are cumulative, so each one covers the tax year so far. Under the standard periods:

You can choose calendar quarters ending 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March instead, with the same deadlines. You still need to send an update if a property was empty and nothing happened in the period. For 2026 to 2027, late quarterly updates do not attract penalty points, but from 2027 to 2028 each late update earns a point and four points bring a £200 penalty. The rules are set out in HMRC's page on sending quarterly updates.

End of year return

After the tax year ends, you make any adjustments, such as claiming allowances, adding jointly let expenses you left out of quarterly updates, or correcting earlier figures. You then submit your tax return through compatible software by 31 January, as now. For landlords who joined in April 2026, that means the 2026 to 2027 return is due by 31 January 2028.

Common landlord questions

Letting agents

Using a letting agent does not take you out of MTD, and the agent's fees do not reduce your qualifying income. If the agent collects £2,000 rent and pays you £1,800 after a 10% fee, your gross rent is £2,000 and the £200 is an expense.

In practice, agent statements become the source for your digital records. Ask your agent how quickly monthly statements arrive and whether they can be exported, so you can record rent, fees and any repairs the agent paid before each quarterly deadline. A letting agent is not the same as a tax agent: unless you have authorised them with HMRC to act for you on MTD, the quarterly updates remain your responsibility.

Rent a Room and small landlords

The Rent a Room scheme lets you earn up to £7,500 a year tax free from letting furnished accommodation in your own home, or £3,750 if the income is shared with someone else, such as a joint owner. If you are already in MTD because of other property or trading income, HMRC says you need to keep digital records of your Rent a Room income too, and the relief can be applied through your quarterly updates or claimed after the year ends.

The £1,000 property income allowance works alongside this for very small lets, such as renting out a driveway. The total allowance cannot exceed £1,000 across all your property businesses, and you cannot use it on the same income you claim Rent a Room relief on. Under MTD the allowance is claimed in your software after the year ends, before you submit the return. If your property income was below the allowance and was not declared on your previous return, you do not need to keep digital records of it.

How to prepare

  1. Work out your qualifying income. Take gross rent, your share only for joint lets, plus any self-employment turnover from your 2025 to 2026 figures. Compare it with £30,000 and, looking ahead, £20,000.
  2. Map your property businesses. List your UK lets and any overseas lets separately, plus any trade, so you know how many sets of updates you will send.
  3. Separate the money. A dedicated account for rent and property costs makes digital records far easier than picking rental items out of a personal account.
  4. Agree arrangements with co-owners and agents. Decide who records what, how shares are split, and when agent statements will arrive.
  5. Choose compatible software. HMRC publishes a list of software that works with MTD. Check it handles UK and foreign property if you need both.

FAQ

Does MTD for landlords use rental profit or gross rent?

Gross rent. Qualifying income is income before expenses, so mortgage interest, repairs and agent fees do not reduce it.

My spouse and I own a buy to let jointly. Do we both join?

Each of you is assessed separately on your own share plus any other property or self-employment income. One of you may be in MTD while the other is not.

Do I need separate quarterly updates for each property?

No for UK lets, which count as one UK property business. Overseas lets form a separate foreign property business with its own updates, although each foreign property needs its own records.

What happens to my former furnished holiday let?

Since 6 April 2025 it is taxed as an ordinary residential let and reported as part of your UK or foreign property business.

Can I leave MTD if my rental income falls?

If your qualifying income stays below the threshold for three tax years in a row, you can stop using MTD. A property sale part way through a year does not automatically take you out if you have other continuing property or self-employment income.
