The next Self Assessment deadline most people face is 31 January 2027, when the online return for the 2025/26 tax year is due and any tax owed must be paid. For the 2026/27 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027), the online tax return deadline is 31 January 2028, or 31 October 2027 if you file on paper. If you are newly self-employed in 2026/27, you need to register with HMRC by 5 October 2027.

Registering for Self Assessment: 5 October

If you started working for yourself during the 2026/27 tax year, you must tell HMRC by 5 October 2027. The same applies if you had other untaxed income for the first time, such as rental income or a large amount of savings interest above your allowances.

If you are already registered and have filed before, you do not need to register again. You will usually get a notice to file each year.

If you left a job part way through the year to work for yourself, keep the P45 your employer gave you. It shows your pay and the tax already deducted, and you will need those figures for your return.

Filing your return

The tax return deadline in the UK depends on how you file. Filing online gives you three extra months.

Paper returns: 31 October

Paper returns for 2026/27 must reach HMRC by 31 October 2027. Few people still file on paper, but if you do, post it with enough time for it to arrive, not just to be sent.

Online returns: 31 January

When is the Self Assessment deadline for online filing? For 2025/26 it is 31 January 2027. For 2026/27 it is 31 January 2028.

The January deadline is also the payment deadline. Filing early, from 6 April onwards, tells you what you owe months in advance.

Collecting tax through your tax code: 30 December

If you also have a job or a pension taxed through PAYE, and you owe less than £3,000, HMRC can collect the tax through your tax code over the following year instead of asking for a lump sum. To use this option, file your 2025/26 return online by 30 December 2026, or your 2026/27 return by 30 December 2027.

Paying your tax

Self Assessment payments fall on two dates each year: 31 January and 31 July. Which payments you make depends on whether you pay payments on account.

Balancing payment and first payment on account

By 31 January 2028 you pay:

  • the balancing payment for 2026/27, which is any tax still owed after your payments on account for that year
  • the first payment on account for 2027/28, if you need to make one

Payments on account are advance payments towards your next tax bill. Each one is usually half of your previous year's bill. You do not have to make them if your last Self Assessment bill was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was already collected at source, for example through PAYE.

The first year of self-employment often brings the biggest January bill. You pay the full tax for your first year plus the first payment on account for the next year at the same time.

If you expect your profits to fall, you can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account. If you reduce them too far, you will be charged interest on the shortfall.

Second payment on account: 31 July

The second payment on account for 2026/27 is due by 31 July 2027. The second payment on account for 2027/28 is due by 31 July 2028. It is normally the same amount as the January payment on account.

Extra dates if you use MTD

From 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. They keep digital records and send HMRC a quarterly update of income and expenses, then submit a final declaration after the tax year ends instead of a standard return.

The MTD quarterly update deadlines for 2026/27 are:

  1. 7 August 2026, covering 6 April to 5 July 2026
  2. 7 November 2026, covering 6 April to 5 October 2026
  3. 7 February 2027, covering 6 April 2026 to 5 January 2027
  4. 7 May 2027, covering 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027

Each update is cumulative, so it covers the tax year to date rather than just the latest three months. The final declaration for 2026/27 is due by 31 January 2028, the same date as the online return. Payment dates do not change under MTD. If you also file VAT returns, this list of Making Tax Digital deadlines includes the VAT dates.

For 2026/27, HMRC says it will not issue penalty points for late quarterly updates. From 6 April 2027, each missed quarterly deadline earns a point.

Penalties for missing a deadline

For most people, the 2025/26 return due on 31 January 2027 falls under the existing penalty rules. If you joined MTD from 6 April 2026, a new points based system applies from the 2026/27 tax year onwards, but your 2025/26 return still uses the existing rules. HMRC's guide to penalties for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax covers the new system in detail.

Late filing

Under the existing rules, explained on GOV.UK's Self Assessment penalties page, a late return costs you:

  • an initial £100 penalty, even if you owe no tax or pay on time
  • after 3 months, £10 a day for up to 90 days, so up to £900
  • after 6 months, a further 5% of the tax due or £300, whichever is greater
  • after 12 months, another 5% or £300, whichever is greater

Under the MTD system, a late return earns one penalty point instead. Once you reach the threshold of 4 points, you get a £200 penalty, and a further £200 for each later missed deadline while you are at the threshold.

Late payment and interest

Under the existing rules, late payment penalties are 5% of the tax unpaid at 30 days, again at 6 months and again at 12 months.

Under the MTD system for 2026/27, there is no penalty if you pay within 15 days. Tax still unpaid after day 15 attracts a 3% penalty, and a further 3% applies to tax still unpaid at day 30. From day 31, a penalty at an annual rate of 10% is charged daily on the outstanding amount. In your first year of the new penalties, the first 3% does not apply if you pay within 30 days. From 2027/28, the two 3% charges rise to 4%.

On top of any penalty, HMRC charges interest from the day after the deadline until you pay. Since 6 April 2025, the late payment rate has been set at the Bank of England base rate plus 4%, up from plus 2.5%. At the time of writing, the rate is 7.75%, in force since 9 January 2026. The rate moves with the base rate, so check HMRC's interest rates for late and early payments for the current figure.

If you cannot pay on time

File your return on time even if you cannot pay, as late filing penalties are separate from late payment penalties.

Then look at a Time to Pay arrangement, which lets you clear the bill in monthly instalments. You can set up a Self Assessment payment plan online if you have filed your return and owe £30,000 or less. If you owe more, or the online service says you are not eligible, you may still be able to agree a plan by phone. HMRC checks that the plan is affordable before agreeing it. See GOV.UK's guide on what to do if you cannot pay your tax bill on time.

Interest still runs on the unpaid tax while you pay, so the sooner you agree a plan and start paying, the less it costs.

FAQ

When is the Self Assessment deadline for 2026?

There is no Self Assessment filing deadline left in 2026 for most people, apart from 30 December 2026 if you want tax collected through your tax code. The next main deadline is 31 January 2027, for the 2025/26 online return and payment. MTD users also have a quarterly update due on 7 November 2026.

What are the Self Assessment deadlines for 2026/27?

Register by 5 October 2027, file a paper return by 31 October 2027, file online by 31 January 2028 and pay by 31 January 2028. The second payment on account for 2026/27 is due by 31 July 2027, and the second payment on account for 2027/28 by 31 July 2028.

Do I get a penalty if I file late but owe no tax?

Yes, under the existing rules. The £100 late filing penalty applies even if you owe nothing.

Can I file my tax return early?

Yes. You can file your 2026/27 return any time from 6 April 2027. Filing early does not mean paying early: the tax is still due by 31 January 2028.

Does MTD change the tax return deadline in the UK?

No. Under MTD, the final declaration is due by 31 January after the end of the tax year, the same as the online return. What MTD adds is the four quarterly updates during the year.
